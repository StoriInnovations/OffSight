3a

Colour in the menu bar

The icon and glyphs have no status colours today. Only the app icon uses the teal accent. Colour can be added as an optional reinforcement, applied to the badge only. The iris itself stays monochrome, so each state still reads by shape.

Live: red dot. Yes. This is the universal "on air" or recording signal, and it's the one state that should pull your eye.
Unprotected: amber, not yellow. Pure yellow almost disappears on a light menu bar, so use a deeper amber there.
Protected: brand teal, not green. macOS already shows a green dot to mean "camera is on". A green Protected would say the opposite of what it means. Teal also ties Protected back to the app icon. I'd still keep it monochrome by default, because it's the everyday state and a permanently coloured icon becomes noise.
Idle and Paused stay monochrome. Paused is a choice you made, not an alarm.

How to ship it: keep the template glyphs from 2a as the default. Turn on colour badges with a "Coloured status" setting, using these non-template images with separate light and dark versions. Red and green look alike to colour-blind users, so red is never the only cue: the open iris and dot still say Live.

Recommendation
Take 1a Iris forward.

It says "shutter" most directly — the mechanism itself, closed — with no lock, shield or eye. Its states separate cleanly at 18 pt: a thin pinwheel (idle) versus a solid disc with a hexagonal opening (live), so Live never depends on colour. It's rotationally symmetric, so a mirrored self-view only reverses the blade spin. The teal accent has the most contrast headroom on slate.

Carry forward from 1b: the knurled metal rim, which gives a stronger "well-made object" feel. From 1c: the softer tone of voice for onboarding and marketing illustration. Next round: tune the blade curvature for ownability, hint the 16 px master by hand, and outline the wordmark.
